Proof of onward travel for Thailand

Thailand is a popular first stop for longer trips through Southeast Asia, and many travellers arrive on a one-way ticket. If you enter without a visa or on a short-stay visa, you may be asked to show that you intend to leave the country. This guide covers who may ask, what an unticketed flight reservation can and cannot do, and how to plan around it.

What is usually required

Thailand's entry rules, including visa exemption periods and eligible nationalities, have changed several times in recent years and may change again. Travellers entering for tourism are generally expected to be able to show:

  • a passport valid for the period required by Thai rules;
  • proof of onward or return travel out of Thailand within the permitted stay;
  • details of where they will stay, and in some cases evidence of sufficient funds;
  • any arrival registration or visa that applies to their nationality.

Proof of onward travel may be checked by the airline at check-in, before a boarding pass is issued for a flight to Thailand, or by immigration on arrival. Airline checks are common because carriers can be responsible for passengers refused entry. Airlines usually rely on IATA Timatic; ask your airline what it will check for your nationality and route, and read the current information published by the Thai authorities or a Royal Thai Embassy or Consulate.

What it is not

A reservation is not a flight ticket. It is unpaid, has no e-ticket number and cannot be used to board. It does not extend your permitted stay, it is not a visa, and it should not be described as a paid ticket to an airline or an officer. When you leave Thailand, you will need to buy a real ticket or other transport.

Do I need proof of onward travel to enter Thailand?

Travellers entering without a visa or on a tourist visa are generally expected to be able to show onward or return travel, and airlines may check this before boarding. Rules vary by nationality and change over time, so check current official information and ask your airline.

Will a flight reservation be accepted at check-in?

It depends on the airline and the agent. Some accept an itinerary with a booking reference, while others ask for an e-ticket number, which a reservation does not have. Ask your airline in advance.

Can I show a bus or train ticket instead?

Some travellers leave Thailand overland, but whether an airline or officer accepts that as proof of onward travel is up to them. Check with your airline before you fly.

How long is the reservation valid?

Typically 24 to 72 hours, depending on the airline. The 7-day and 14-day plans renew it automatically and e-mail you a new PNR each time.

Can I fly out of Thailand with the reservation?

No. It is not a ticket and cannot be used to board. Buy a ticket for the flight you actually take.
